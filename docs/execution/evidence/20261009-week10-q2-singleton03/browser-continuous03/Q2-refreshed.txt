How do light-dependent reactions provide the energy carriers used by the Calvin cycle?
Learning Assistant
Live model
·
Intermediate profile applied
Textbook sources

The light-dependent reactions convert light energy into chemical energy [5] by using photosystems and electron transport chains to produce the energy carriers ATP and NADPH [2] [6]. These energy carriers are considered "full" because they transport this stored energy from the light-dependent reactions into the stroma where the Calvin cycle reactions take place [1] [3] [4]. Once the energy is released for the Calvin cycle, the "empty" energy carriers return to the light-dependent reactions to obtain more energy [1] [3].
