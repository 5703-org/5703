### Complete delivered answer

> Diffusion is the movement of substances from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration, and a space with a different concentration of a single substance is said to have a concentration gradient [ev_002]. Osmosis is the movement of free water molecules through a semipermeable membrane according to the water's concentration gradient across the membrane, which is inversely proportional to the solute's concentration [ev_004]. Osmosis is a special case of diffusion [ev_001].
>
> Concentration gradients: each separate substance in a medium has its own concentration gradient, independent of the concentration gradients of other materials, and each substance diffuses according to that gradient [ev_014]. In osmosis, water has a concentration gradient in the system and diffuses down its own concentration gradient, crossing the membrane to the side where water is less concentrated [ev_008].
>
> Membrane permeability: in diffusion across a membrane, only the material capable of getting through the membrane will diffuse through it [ev_008]. Whereas diffusion transports material across membranes and within cells, osmosis transports only water across a membrane, and the membrane limits the diffusion of solutes in the water [ev_001].
>
> Water movement: water moves from an area of high concentration of free water molecules to one of low free water molecule concentration [ev_001]. In osmosis, water always moves from an area of higher water concentration to one of lower water concentration, and this diffusion of water through the membrane continues until the water concentration gradient goes to zero [ev_008].

Complete delivered short answer:

> Diffusion moves substances from higher to lower concentration along a concentration gradient [ev_002], and each substance diffuses according to its own independent gradient [ev_014]. Osmosis is a special case of diffusion in which only water crosses a semipermeable membrane, moving from higher to lower free-water concentration along the water's concentration gradient, which is inversely proportional to solute concentration [ev_001] [ev_004].
