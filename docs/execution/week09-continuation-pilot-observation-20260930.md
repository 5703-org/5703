# Week 9 continuation: source-frozen development pilot

The 30 September development pilot used a frozen source snapshot (`ff7d23bd0b9bdab17e624acad11878141a360ed2ecd4a73e5676e338f6ecc139`), the isolated PostgreSQL clone with 10,594 real E5 vectors, the published four-book OpenStax release, and a live DeepSeek `deepseek-flash` configuration. Fifteen new source-anchored cases across four concept groups produced all 21 planned terminal observations. The pilot was a development gate. It did not execute the reserved study, establish a teaching effect, or include human ratings. The exact sanitized counts, hashes, and cost ledger are in [the pilot receipt](../../evidence/week09-continuation/20260930/pilot-v2-sanitized.json).

Its eight direct teaching calls failed at the runner's network boundary before provider usage could be established. The same frozen candidate was therefore evaluated in an independent network-enabled `pilot-run-v2`. Neither the v1 product failures nor the v1 runner failures were erased or pooled with v2.

Human review is still needed to decide whether any rejected draft was substantively safe.

## Independent scoring and study gate

The offline AI judge was blinded to the experimental arm and online check. It shares the provider/model family with the generator and is not a human rater. Of 11 packets with a visible learner response, it returned 7 typed ratings and 4 invalid JSON outputs with duplicate keys; 10 other packets had no visible output and made no judge call. The seven AI ratings are a scored subset, not a complete answer or tutoring rate. For A, B, and C, one of two delivered hints lacked a valid rating. D had one scored hint and one terminal failure. The primary four-arm comparison therefore has incomplete independent labels.

The prespecified 55-packet judge calibration generated 38 valid ratings and 17 invalid outputs. Among evaluable paired ratings, agreement on the primary fields was 4/4 for source order, 5/5 for a neutral length change, 5/5 for wording frame, and 4/4 for an untrusted instruction. Those small valid subsets met the frozen calibration thresholds. They do not remove the 17 invalid outputs. The frozen pilot decision is `ready_for_reserved=false`; the nominal 16-concept-group estimate is a planning floor, not a formal power result. The reserved catalogue remains unopened for outcome execution.

A five-persona simulated-student calibration was attempted after v2. All five rows failed before a provider call because its runner invoked `LLMAdapter.generate` without required schema arguments. The original error rows remain private; the corrected runner calls `generate_basic` and requires a new versioned execution before any simulated-student result can be interpreted. No multi-turn trajectory or real-student learning effect was measured.

## Calls, latency, and estimated cost

The v2 product outcomes account for 34 provider calls with outcome-level usage and an estimated USD 0.02891873. The two practice proposal audit records add two calls and USD 0.00089688, yielding a USD 0.02981561 product subtotal. The offline judge cost estimate is USD 0.01570965, and the 55 calibration calls add USD 0.07767840. The known v2 total is USD 0.12320366. Programmed clarifications, refusals, and the access block made zero model calls even where their provider token fields are non-applicable. These are estimates from recorded cache-hit, cache-miss, and output tokens, not provider billing. The source-frozen product calls ran around 19:32–19:38 UTC on 29 September; judging began around 19:39 UTC, within the official DeepSeek off-peak period. The [official pricing page](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing/) listed USD 0.003 per million cache-hit input tokens, USD 0.15 per million cache-miss input tokens, and USD 0.60 per million output tokens for `deepseek-flash` / DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash when checked on 30 September local time.

After these records were sealed, the offline judge gained a bounded strict-JSON retry and full attempt ledger, the simulator gained its valid basic-text call, and the memory pilot adapter gained the production model's tokenizer. These changes belong to a new candidate. They do not alter any v1/v2 outcomes, independent ratings, calibration result, or the `NO-GO` decision.
