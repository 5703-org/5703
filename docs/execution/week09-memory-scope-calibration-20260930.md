# Week 9 local memory-scope calibration and study-scope audit

The [sanitized selection receipt](../../evidence/week09-continuation/20260930/memory-v3-scope-calibration-sanitized.json) records a new, bounded offline study of the optional `query_conditioned_memory_v3` selector. The 40 synthetic preference/question pairs were authored and frozen before running the pinned `intfloat/e5-small-v2` model on CPU. They are split by concept into five development groups (20 pairs) and five held-out groups (20 pairs). The exact authored questions, memory contents, labels, per-case traces and original calibration files remain outside the distributable project under `E:/5703/week09-private-db/memory-selector-v3-calibration-20260930/study-v2/`. They contain no real learner records. The reference applicability labels are author judgments, with **zero independent human ratings**.

The runner freezes the catalogue and source hashes, searches only the development split over the predeclared 0.50–1.00 cutoff grid, then evaluates the held-out split once under the selected diagnostic cutoff. The first runner version terminated on the development gate before accessing held-out cases; the successor kept the identical authored catalogue and reports a rejected gate explicitly. No questions, labels or group assignments were changed after the first run. The actual selector returned `semantic_supplement` for 12 development and 11 held-out cases, and `rules_sufficient` for the remaining eight and nine. There were 23 real CPU E5 encoding batches, zero answer-model calls and no database writes.

No cutoff in the frozen grid eliminated the three development false selections. They entered through the existing broad topic-rule path, which the optional semantic supplement does not override. Lower cutoffs added selections and false selections; at 0.50–0.80, every development negative was selected. Cutoff 1.00 minimizes observed false selections and is recorded **only for diagnosis**. The calibration decision is `rejected_development_false_selection`; no activation-ready policy file was emitted and the product default remains rules-only. A future candidate needs a versioned rule/semantic decision change, a new development set, independent applicability review and a new untouched holdout. This study does not measure whether selected preferences affected answers, practice or revision.
